Online retail customer analytics

Python · pandas · scikit-learn · 541,909 transactions · 5 tests
Question
Who are a UK gift wholesaler's most valuable customers, do new customers come back, and what gets bought together?
Data
A year of real transactions from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, Dec 2010 – Dec 2011.
Method
A tested cleaning pipeline, RFM segmentation, monthly cohort retention, k-means clustering chosen by silhouette score, a Lorenz curve, and market-basket lift.
Result
The top 20% of customers bring in 74% of revenue. Only 21% of new customers buy again the following month. 347 lapsed "can't lose them" accounts hold £630k of past revenue, making them the first win-back target. Cleaning also caught £424k of orders that were placed and then cancelled, including a single 80,995-unit order, all of which would otherwise have counted as sales.

Equity factor research

Python · backtesting · 97 US stocks · 2015–2025 · 6 tests
Question
Do momentum, short-term reversal and low-volatility still earn a premium in US large caps once costs are counted?
Data
Eleven years of daily adjusted prices with GICS sector labels.
Method
Monthly quintile long-short portfolios, raw and sector-neutral, measured by information coefficient, turnover, 10 bps trading costs and a strict in-sample / out-of-sample split. Tests check for look-ahead bias.
Result
No, at least not here. Momentum looked fine in-sample (Sharpe 0.24) and lost 8% a year out-of-sample. Reversal had a real signal (gross Sharpe 0.45) that trading costs wiped out. The write-up explains how survivorship bias in a "today's large caps" universe tilts the results.

SQL business analytics

SQL · SQLite · 15 queries · CTEs · window functions
Question
Fifteen questions a music store's management would ask: growth, markets, genres, customer value, cross-sell and data quality.
Data
The Chinook database: customers, invoices, tracks, artists and staff, 2021–2025.
Method
Pure SQL, one file per question, using LAG, RANK, NTILE, ROW_NUMBER, rolling windows, recursive CTEs, anti-joins and reconciliation checks.
Result
Revenue is flat at about $450–480 a year (it is a small sample database). 43% of the catalogue has never sold. Revenue per customer is almost the same in every country. The data itself is too regular to be real (every customer has exactly 7 orders), and the write-up says so rather than over-reading it.

Kenya's economy vs East African peers

Python · World Bank API · 12 indicators · 2000–2024
Question
How do Kenya's inflation, growth, shilling and external position compare with Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda and Ethiopia?
Data
About 1,300 country-year observations pulled straight from the World Bank API.
Method
Peer profiles, real income indexed to 2000, regressions of exchange-rate pass-through, and an automatic check for breaks in the data series.
Result
Kenya is the richest economy in the group but has the slowest growth in income per person: 1.9% a year, against 4.9–5.4% for Rwanda and Ethiopia. Inflation has stayed in the central bank's target band in 9 of the last 12 years. Diaspora remittances now cover 66% of the trade deficit, up from 13% in 2011.

Kenya climate trends, 1981–2025

Python · ERA5 reanalysis · 82,180 city-days · Mann-Kendall
Question
How fast are Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret and Garissa warming, and how reliable are the two rainy seasons?
Data
45 years of daily temperature and rainfall from the Open-Meteo archive.
Method
OLS and Mann-Kendall trend tests against a 1981–2010 baseline, seasonal rainfall anomalies, failed-season counts, and hot days above each city's own 90th percentile.
Result
All five cities are warming at 0.18–0.27 °C per decade (p < 0.001). Nairobi's hot days rose from about 30 to 77 a year. Rainfall swings widely and shows no clear trend in Nairobi. The write-up lists which rainfall results not to trust, and why.

Alpha research on WorldQuant BRAIN

Quantitative research · market data · 2023 – now
Question
Which combinations of price, volume and fundamental fields predict next-period returns well enough to survive out-of-sample?
Data
BRAIN's equity datasets: daily price and volume, fundamentals, and analyst fields across a large universe of stocks.
Method
Wrote signal expressions, ran simulations, then neutralised, decayed and ranked them to control turnover and sector exposure. Rejected anything too correlated with signals already in the pool.
Takeaway
Most ideas fail. The useful skill was reading a backtest critically and spotting overfitting before submitting, not finding one lucky formula.

School exam results system

Python · Django · SQL (via ORM) · 15 tests
Question
How can a Kenyan secondary school get correct grades, means and class positions out of hundreds of mark sheets without spreadsheet errors?
Data
Students, subjects, classes and per-teacher mark entry for each exam, stored in a relational schema.
Method
KCSE-style grading (A = 12 points down to E = 1). Calculates totals, mean points, mean grade, and rank by class and by whole form, with ties sharing a position and missing marks handled explicitly. Marks are validated to 0–100 and lock when an exam is published.
Output
Class and form result tables, plus printable report forms for each student. The ranking edge cases are covered by tests.

Rubric-based evaluation of AI model output

Data quality · annotation · remote contract work
Question
Is this model response correct, complete and safe, and how does it compare with an alternative response?
Data
Large batches of prompt and response pairs covering code, maths and reasoning.
Method
Scored responses against written rubrics on Likert scales, ranked pairs, checked facts and code by hand, and gave every score a written reason someone else could audit.
Takeaway
Consistent labelling is a statistics problem. Clear criteria and written justifications are what make a dataset trustworthy.
